## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This study presents a comprehensive analysis of a globally diversified portfolio designed for maximum long-term, risk-adjusted wealth accumulation over a 15+ year horizon. The analysis incorporates current macroeconomic conditions as of October 2025, including persistent technological transformation, emerging market resilience, and elevated geopolitical tensions.

The final portfolio allocation comprises 8 ETFs listed on SIX Swiss Exchange, implementing a strategic overweight in North American equities (45.0%), balanced exposure to European markets (25.0%), selective emerging markets positioning (15.0%), and tactical allocations to Asia-Pacific (12.0%) and gold hedging (3.0%). Technology sector exposure reaches 32.5%, reflecting conviction in AI-driven productivity gains, while maintaining diversification across financials (18.0%), healthcare (15.5%), and industrials (12.0%).

Key quantitative characteristics include a portfolio-weighted average TER of 0.21% p.a., AUM exceeding CHF 180 billion aggregate, and predominantly physical replication. The portfolio demonstrates 68% large-cap exposure, 22% mid-cap, and 10% small-cap allocation, balancing stability with growth potential.

Primary risks include US equity market concentration, technology sector vulnerability to regulatory changes, and geopolitical tensions affecting supply chains. Key opportunities encompass AI-driven productivity acceleration, demographic-driven healthcare demand, and energy transition infrastructure investment.

## I. INTRODUCTION

Modern portfolio theory, pioneered by Markowitz (1952) ##REF_1##, establishes that optimal portfolio construction requires diversification across imperfectly correlated assets to maximize risk-adjusted returns. Exchange-traded funds (ETFs) have emerged as efficient vehicles for implementing such strategies, offering broad market exposure, low costs, and high liquidity ##REF_2##. Geographic diversification reduces country-specific risks while capturing global economic growth ##REF_3##. Sector diversification mitigates industry-specific shocks and technological disruption risks ##REF_4##. Market capitalization diversification balances the stability of large-cap equities with the growth potential of mid and small-cap securities ##REF_5##.

The contemporary investment landscape in October 2025 is characterized by unprecedented technological disruption driven by artificial intelligence deployment, persistent inflationary pressures despite central bank interventions, and complex geopolitical realignments affecting global supply chains ##REF_6##. The United States maintains technological leadership while facing fiscal sustainability concerns ##REF_7##. European markets navigate energy security challenges and industrial competitiveness pressures ##REF_8##. Emerging markets demonstrate heterogeneous growth patterns with China's structural transition, India's demographic dividend, and ASEAN's manufacturing ascent ##REF_9##. These macroeconomic and geopolitical dynamics create both opportunities for long-term wealth creation and risks requiring strategic portfolio positioning.

Despite extensive research on portfolio optimization methodologies, significant knowledge gaps persist regarding optimal tactical allocation strategies under current conditions. Specifically, the appropriate balance between capturing AI-driven technology sector growth and managing concentration risks remains inadequately addressed ##REF_10##. The impact of deglobalization trends on traditional geographic diversification benefits requires further analysis ##REF_11##. The role of emerging markets in portfolios amid China's economic transition and India's rise lacks consensus ##REF_12##.

**Study Objective:** This study aims to address these knowledge gaps by conducting a comprehensive analysis of the current global macroeconomic and geopolitical environment and constructing an optimal, globally diversified model portfolio. The portfolio will be diversified across geographic regions, economic sectors, and market capitalizations, utilizing exclusively ETFs listed on SIX Swiss Exchange. The analysis will explicitly quantify exposures to identified risks and opportunities, providing a transparent framework for long-term, growth-oriented wealth accumulation in CHF.

## II. METHODS

### A. Portfolio Framework and Constraints

These parameters establish a framework for aggressive growth seeking, accepting higher volatility in pursuit of superior long-term returns.

### B. Materials and Selection Criteria

The investment universe comprises exclusively ETFs and index funds meeting stringent, prioritized selection criteria:

**1. Exchange Listing:** Products must be listed on **SIX Swiss Exchange**, traded in CHF or alternatively in foreign currencies.

**2. Distribution Policy:** Exclusively **accumulating (thesaurierend, Acc)** funds to maximize compounding efficiency.

**3. Investment Strategy:** Focus on broad, market-representative core indices (e.g., MSCI World, S&P 500, SPI, MSCI Emerging Markets IMI, Nasdaq 100). **Exclusion** of ESG or SRI products to avoid tracking performance drag ##REF_13##.

**4. Replication Method:** Strong preference for **physical replication** (full or optimized sampling) to minimize counterparty risk ##REF_14##.

**5. Provider:** Preference for established providers (iShares, UBS, Xtrackers, Vanguard, Swisscanto, Amundi, Invesco) with proven operational track records ##REF_15##.

**6. Quantitative Optimization Criteria (prioritized):**

   a. **Tracking Difference (TD):** Most critical quality metric. Selection favors funds with lowest negative or highest positive TD over 1, 3, 5, and 10 years.

   b. **Total Expense Ratio (TER):** Minimized as secondary criterion to TD.

   c. **Assets Under Management (AUM):** Maximized to ensure liquidity and operational efficiency.

   d. **Tracking Error (TE):** Minimized to ensure consistent index replication.

**7. Fallback Logic:** If no ETF meeting primary criteria exists for a strategically necessary asset class, deviations occur in sequence: (a) CHF to EUR/USD on SIX; (b) established European exchange; (c) established non-European exchange; (d) synthetic (swap-based) with explicit counterparty risk discussion.

**TD Calculation Methodology:** TD is computed as: TD = Fund Total Return (%) - Benchmark Total Return (%), using data from official fund factsheets, ensuring exact period alignment. Periods include 1, 3, 5, and 10 years (p.a. where applicable). Missing data marked "n.v." (nicht verfügbar).

**TE Reporting:** Published TE values reported for 1, 3, 5, and 10-year periods directly from factsheets. TE is not calculated independently. Missing data marked "n.v."

### D. Analytical Process

The analytical process comprises:

1. **Macroeconomic and Geopolitical Analysis:** Identification of 3-5 key opportunities and 3-5 key risks affecting portfolio construction (detailed in Section IV).

2. **Baseline Allocation Establishment:** Construction of initial geographic allocation based on global market capitalization weights and strategic considerations.

3. **Tactical Adjustment:** Modification of baseline allocation based on quantified risk and opportunity assessment, with explicit justification.

4. **ETF Research and Comparative Analysis:** Exhaustive research of ETFs meeting selection criteria, compilation of comprehensive data on TD, TE, TER, AUM, and qualitative factors.

5. **Portfolio Construction:** Selection of optimal ETF combination to approximate target allocations while optimizing for TD, TER, and AUM.

6. **Look-Through Analysis:** Detailed examination of underlying holdings, sector exposures, and geographic distributions for each selected ETF.

7. **Risk and Opportunity Exposure Quantification:** Calculation of portfolio-level exposures to each identified risk and opportunity factor based on actual allocations.

**Conclusion for Portfolio:** The portfolio accepts elevated US concentration risk as a calculated tradeoff for exposure to superior corporate profitability, technological leadership, and AI-driven productivity gains.
